Skilled workers in Nigeria get paid across many banks, wallets, and jobs. A bricklayer might receive a transfer from a contractor on Monday, a part payment on Wednesday, and cash on Saturday. At the end of the month, a bank statement only lists deposits. A loan officer cannot tell whether a transfer came from a paying employer, a family member, or another loan.

Provo solves this by tying payment directly to confirmed daily work. Before a job starts, the employer sends the full job pay into a one-time 10-digit bank account number created for that job. Each day the worker finishes, the employer or a site supervisor confirms the day. Provo then sends that day's pay to the worker's Paga account and adds a verified day to their work record.

<Note>
**Provo** holds job pay safely before Day 1 begins, pays the worker day by day as work is confirmed, and turns settled jobs into a signed work report that lenders can trust.
</Note>

## Why lenders trust Provo reports

Across Nigeria, payment access rose from 22% to 52% of adults between 2010 and 2023, while credit access grew by roughly 4 percentage points (EFInA, 2023). Across Africa, around 83% of jobs are informal (ILO and UNECA, 2024). Workers have mobile wallets, yet wallets alone do not prove steady work.

- **Works for any lender:** Any microfinance bank or fintech can read a worker's Provo report, regardless of which Nigerian bank funded the jobs.
- **Pay from any bank:** Employers fund each job with a normal bank transfer from OPay, UBA, Access, GTBank, Kuda, or Paga Checkout.
- **Funded upfront:** Locking the full job amount before Day 1 proves the money exists before work starts.
- **Clear daily outcomes:** Every day records what happened (`CONFIRMED`, `AUTO_RELEASED`, `DISPUTED`, or `PAID`), along with the trade, number of days, and receipt number.
