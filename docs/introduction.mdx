Skilled tradespeople in Nigeria get paid across many banks, wallets, and jobs. A bricklayer might receive a transfer from a contractor on Monday, a partial bank payment on Wednesday, and cash on Saturday. At the end of the quarter, a bank statement only shows deposits. A loan officer cannot tell whether a transfer came from a paying client, a family member, or a short-term loan.

Provo solves this by tying payment directly to confirmed daily work. Before a job starts, the client locks the full job total into a one-time Paga virtual account. Each day the worker finishes, the client or an assigned site supervisor confirms the day, releasing that day's pay to the worker's Paga account and recording a verified work entry.

<Note>
**Provo** holds job pay in Paga escrow before Day 1 begins, disburses pay day by day upon confirmation, and compiles settled jobs into a signed credibility report that lenders can verify.
</Note>

## Why Provo works for underwriting

Across Nigeria, payment access rose from 22% to 52% of adults between 2010 and 2023, while credit access grew by roughly 4 percentage points (EFInA, 2023). Across Africa, around 83% of jobs are informal (ILO and UNECA, 2024). Workers have mobile wallets, yet wallets alone do not prove steady employment.

- **Lender-neutral:** Any microfinance bank or fintech can read a worker's Provo report regardless of which Nigerian bank funded the jobs.
- **Works from any bank:** Clients fund each job via standard NIBSS NIP transfer from OPay, UBA, Access, GTBank, Kuda, or Paga Checkout.
- **Funded upfront:** Locking `total_kobo` before Day 1 proves the job budget exists before work begins.
- **Structured daily outcomes:** Every day records an explicit status (`CONFIRMED`, `AUTO_RELEASED`, `DISPUTED`, or `PAID`), along with the trade, duration, and receipt ID.
